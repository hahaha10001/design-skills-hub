# Composition Modes

Choose the mode that matches the user's job. Combine modes only when the product clearly needs two operating surfaces.

## Command Center

Best for monitoring, dispatch, incident response, ops, analytics, and admin workflows.

- Put live status, next action, and filters in the first viewport.
- Use dense but stable grids.
- Keep repeated actions in predictable locations.
- Avoid oversized marketing sections.

## Working Canvas

Best for editors, builders, planning boards, drawing, writing, and arrangement tools.

- Give the artifact most of the viewport.
- Put tools near the object they affect.
- Keep panels collapsible and keyboard-friendly.
- Avoid decorative framing around the work area.

## Narrative Corridor

Best for launches, portfolios, case studies, product explainers, and editorial sites.

- Move from promise to proof to detail.
- Use sectional contrast, not disconnected cards.
- Keep the next section visible from the hero.
- Avoid generic split hero layouts when the subject needs to be seen.

## Comparison Matrix

Best for shopping, vendor selection, plan comparison, audits, and research.

- Make scanning and tradeoffs the main behavior.
- Align attributes across items.
- Keep sort, filters, and explanation close to decisions.
- Avoid card layouts that hide comparable facts.

## Guided Flow

Best for onboarding, checkout, complex forms, setup, booking, and applications.

- Show current step, expected effort, and reversible choices.
- Group fields by user mental model.
- Write error recovery before decorative copy.
- Avoid surprise validation after the user has moved on.

## Spatial Gallery

Best for architecture, fashion, venues, travel, art, food, and object-forward catalogs.

- Let real images or objects establish identity early.
- Use metadata to support inspection.
- Keep cropping honest when the user needs to evaluate the subject.
- Avoid purely atmospheric imagery.
